# Grace Seeds School
# Curriculum planning and teaching records user manual

- For teachers, administrators, Head Teachers and Deputy Head Teachers

- Edition: 17 November 2026

- This manual describes the current web implementation, including local changes that must be released before staff use the new screens.
- It is not confirmation that those changes are already on the live website.
- Mobile and desktop applications are outside this edition.

## 1. The process at a glance

- Published curriculum → School Calendar → Timetable → Syllabus Coverage Plan → Scheme of Work → Lesson Plan → Record of Work → Leadership review → Confirmed coverage

- The four teaching documents are the syllabus coverage plan, scheme of work, lesson plan and record of work.
- Curriculum, calendar, assignments and timetable provide their starting information.

- **Curriculum strands, sub-strands and outcomes**
  - Main responsibility: Leadership maintains the published curriculum; assigned teachers can propose additions or changes
  - Approval or publication: Teacher proposals require leadership approval and publication
- **School Calendar Plan and exception dates**
  - Main responsibility: Administrator, Head Teacher or Deputy Head Teacher
  - Approval or publication: Publish the calendar and subsequent changes
- **Teaching assignments and timetable**
  - Main responsibility: Authorised school leadership
  - Approval or publication: Configure and save for the correct period
- **Syllabus Coverage Plan**
  - Main responsibility: Assigned subject teacher
  - Approval or publication: Submit; leadership approves or returns with comments
- **Scheme of Work**
  - Main responsibility: Assigned subject teacher
  - Approval or publication: Submit completed sessions; leadership approves or returns
- **Lesson Plan**
  - Main responsibility: Assigned subject teacher
  - Approval or publication: Save and use immediately; no approval required
- **Record of Work**
  - Main responsibility: Teacher who taught the lesson
  - Approval or publication: Save draft, submit, then leadership approves or returns


- A sub-strand is not limited to one week.
- It can continue across several teaching sessions and weeks according to the children’s needs.
- A week number identifies a timetable position, not a deadline for finishing a sub-strand.

## 2. Before anyone starts

### Administrator: confirm the school setup

1. Confirm the real operational academic year and term in the academic year and term settings. Check their dates, not only their names.
2. If preparing a future year, select that year in planning. Do not activate the future year just to prepare its plans.
3. Check that each teacher’s login is linked to the correct staff profile.
4. Save class-teacher duties and subject-teacher assignments separately for the correct year, class, stream and learning area. Check effective dates.
5. Confirm published curriculum, the school’s first-use decision, published calendar and matching timetable as described below.

### Teacher: check the teaching context

- Open Curriculum & Timetable and select the relevant planning page.
- Check the class, stream, learning area, academic year and term before saving anything.
- The planning roadmap shows what is ready and the next action.

- A class-teacher duty and a subject assignment have different purposes.
- A teacher may be class teacher for one stream and teach only Science in another.
- Subject planning and marks access follow the subject assignment; learners and guardians access follows the class-teacher duty.
- Use your personal timetable for all your own teaching periods.
- Full class timetables are available for your class-teacher cohorts, not every class where you teach one subject.

## 3. Create and publish curriculum strands

### Leadership: add published curriculum

1. Open CBE Competencies & Grading → Strands & Outcomes.
2. Select the grade and learning area. Check that the entry does not already exist.
3. Use the add-strand control. Enter the strand code, title, grade and any description requested, then save.
4. Open the strand and add its sub-strands. Enter the code, title and specific learning outcomes, one outcome per line.
5. Check the hierarchy: grade → learning area → strand → sub-strand → learning outcomes.
6. Confirm the entries appear under the correct grade and learning area before teachers allocate lessons.

- Use curriculum wording validated by the school.
- Do not add a second strand merely because an existing entry is not visible under the wrong filter.

### Teacher: propose a missing entry

1. Open Strands & Outcomes and choose an assigned learning area and the correct grade.
2. Use the available strand or sub-strand creation/editing form and save the proposed content.
3. Check My curriculum proposals for its review status. A saved teacher proposal is not immediately published curriculum.
4. Read returned comments and provide the corrected proposal through the available form. Do not assume editing the published dictionary bypasses review.
5. Wait for publication, then return to coverage planning and choose Refresh strands.

- If adding a new strand and its children, get the parent strand published first.
- An unpublished proposal cannot be used as a published parent for another entry.

### Leadership: review teacher proposals

1. On Strands & Outcomes select the relevant learning area.
2. Find Curriculum proposals awaiting publication and choose Refresh proposals if necessary.
3. Check the grade, parent strand, code, title and outcomes against the school’s curriculum.
4. Choose Approve & Publish and enter validation comments, or Return with Comments and explain the correction needed.
5. Confirm the published entry appears in the dictionary. Ask the teacher to refresh the curriculum in the coverage screen.

## 4. Set up the School Calendar Plan

- Open Curriculum & Timetable → School Calendar Plan.
- This is the dedicated calendar administration page; teachers are not expected to configure it.

### Select the period and dates

1. Select the academic year and term. Select a class when preparing class-specific exceptions.
2. Check the displayed term start and closing dates.
3. If the current year and current term disagree, read both displayed selections and open the linked settings to correct them.
4. Correct the term start through academic settings where required. The calendar’s start date is read-only.
5. Use the closing-date control where permitted. Deputy Head Teachers do not have that closing-date editor; an authorised administrator or Head Teacher must handle that change.
6. Tick the normal Teaching days. Add any exceptional extra teaching dates in the Additional teaching dates field, using comma-separated ISO dates such as 2026-11-21.

### Record the first term using this system

- If the school has no earlier approved scheme history, choose Start First Term in Grace Seeds School and record the genuine reason.
- This first period can be Term 1, Term 2 or Term 3.
- It does not have to be the first numbered school term.

- If there is approved previous-term history, teachers should review and carry forward unfinished work instead.
- If this is a later period and prior records are missing, investigate the named class, stream, learning area and period.
- Restore genuine records where available, or use the administrator’s scoped missing-prior resolution with a truthful reason.
- Do not create empty or fictional schemes to pass the check.

### Add holidays, closures and activity exceptions

1. Choose Add exception.
2. Enter Date. For a multi-day event, also enter End date.
3. Choose Kind: HOLIDAY, CLOSURE or ACTIVITY.
4. Choose Scope: Whole school or Selected class. For a class exception, confirm the selected class before publishing. This control is class-scoped, not a stream-only selector.
5. For a whole-day exclusion, leave Periods and the time fields empty.
6. For selected periods, enter their numbers, for example 1,2. For a time-based exclusion, enter the start and end times. Prefer one clear method unless the school has checked the combined result.
7. Enter a clear Reason, for example “School sports day — no classroom teaching”.
8. Repeat for other dates. Review all exceptions and choose Publish calendar, or Publish calendar changes if already published.

- Before first publication you can use Save draft calendar.
- A draft is not the published teaching calendar.
- After publication, saved changes must be published to take effect.

- An ACTIVITY exception is a teaching exclusion.
- Use it when the activity removes teaching time; do not enter an ordinary event that leaves lessons unaffected as a whole-day exclusion.

### Example: an activity announced one week beforehand

- Suppose the school announces a Friday trip a week before it happens.
- Open School Calendar Plan, select the actual term, add an ACTIVITY exception for that Friday, choose Whole school or the affected class, leave time and period fields empty for a full-day trip, enter the reason and publish changes.

- Then review affected schedules with teachers.
- The system does not automatically move approved lessons or rewrite teaching evidence.
- Follow the available refresh/rescheduling and review process, preserving approved history.
- Tell teachers about the change directly; do not assume an automatic notification has been sent.

- The dashboard’s “My teaching today” summary does not yet apply all holiday and activity exclusions.
- Use the published calendar and planning schedule when checking an exception; do not treat the dashboard summary alone as proof that a lesson remains valid.

## 5. Set up the timetable

- After publishing the calendar, choose Set Up Timetable.
- You can also use Class Timetable in the sidebar.

1. Confirm the same academic year and term used in the calendar.
2. Select the correct class and stream.
3. Configure the teaching periods, learning areas and responsible teachers using the existing timetable form.
4. Save and check each subject teacher’s own timetable.
5. Confirm that enough usable subject periods remain after holidays, closures and activities.

- Teachers should read timetable readiness, not create their own unofficial periods.
- If no suitable periods exist, leadership must correct the assignment, timetable or calendar.
- Another year’s timetable does not establish readiness for this term.

## 6. Document 1 — Syllabus Coverage Plan

### Teacher: prepare and submit coverage

1. Open Curriculum & Timetable → Syllabus Coverage Plan.
2. Check your assigned class, stream, learning area, year and term.
3. Read the roadmap. If an assignment, calendar or timetable is missing, note the named missing item and contact leadership. You may be able to save a draft before scheduling is ready; submission and scheduled work remain subject to the displayed checks.
4. Review any previous approved unfinished work offered for carry-forward. Select the work genuinely needed for this period; this retains the earlier source instead of changing the old scheme.
5. Open Coverage allocation. Expand a strand, choose the relevant sub-strands and use Add to sequence.
6. Enter Lessons before next sub-strand as a pacing estimate. Arrange the teaching order with the move controls. Check Coverage outcomes.
7. Choose Save coverage. Saving does not submit it.
8. Choose Submit coverage draft for review. Wait for leadership review before submitting scheme sessions.

- You do not have to force all curriculum into the available term.
- Do not use coverage as a week-by-week lesson-writing form.
- Detailed scheduled teaching sessions belong in Scheme of Work.

### Leadership: approve coverage

1. Open the teacher’s matching coverage plan and select Coverage allocation, or open its School leadership review panel in Schemes of Work.
2. Check that the source status is SUBMITTED and that the class, learning area and period are correct.
3. Review curriculum selection, sequencing, lesson estimates and available teaching time.
4. Choose Approve coverage draft / Approve coverage, or Return with comments / Return coverage with comments.
5. On return, explain what to change. The teacher edits the returned draft, saves and resubmits.

- An approved or submitted coverage draft is not freely editable.
- Use the available reopen/review process and provide a reason where requested.
- Reopening does not itself grant approval to revised curriculum.

## 7. Document 2 — Scheme of Work

### Teacher: complete teaching sessions

1. Open Curriculum & Timetable → Schemes of Work, or Open Scheme of Work creation from coverage.
2. Select the matching saved coverage plan. Check its approval status.
3. Choose a Sub-strand and then a Teaching session identified by its real date and period.
4. Complete Specific learning outcomes, Key inquiry questions, Learning experiences, Learning resources and Assessment methods. Enter separate items on separate lines where appropriate.
5. Choose Save teaching session before leaving it or submitting.
6. Choose the Submission scope: the relevant sub-strand or all completed, unapproved lessons.
7. Choose Submit completed lessons for review. You can submit one completed sub-strand’s sessions without finishing the whole term.

- The current minimum completion check includes outcomes, learning experiences and assessment methods as well as strand/sub-strand identity.
- Complete inquiry questions and resources too so the scheme is useful for teaching and document output.

- You can prepare and save scheme drafts before coverage approval, but source coverage must be approved before scheme submission.
- Only approved sessions can be used as approved sources for lesson plans and records.

### When children need more time

1. Keep the current sub-strand selected and choose Continue this sub-strand.
2. Choose the next suitable teaching session and explain why more time is needed.
3. Choose Add teaching session, complete it and submit the new session when ready.
4. Review Deferred session history if another draft topic was moved aside. Plan that unfinished work later.

- The continuation can cross week boundaries.
- Approved sessions and teaching records cannot simply be replaced.
- If there are no suitable periods, ask leadership to review the timetable/calendar rather than overwriting approved work.

### Leadership: approve scheme sessions

1. Open Schemes of Work and the relevant teacher’s scheme.
2. Find School leadership review. Confirm coverage is approved and scheme sessions are SUBMITTED.
3. Read the submitted session content and the number of sessions awaiting review. Approval applies to the submitted sessions, not every unfinished session in the term.
4. Choose Approve scheme, or Return scheme with comments and give specific corrections.
5. The teacher corrects returned sessions, saves and resubmits. Further draft sessions need their own submission even after earlier sessions are approved.

## 8. Document 3 — Lesson Plan

### Teacher: prepare the detailed lesson

1. Open Curriculum & Timetable → Lesson Plans and start creating a plan.
2. Choose an approved scheme and scheduled lesson in the approved-scheme picker. Alternatively, open an approved teaching session in the scheme and choose Create lesson plan from scheme lesson.
3. Check the inherited class, stream, learning area, strand, sub-strand, date, time and curriculum content.
4. Review the outcomes, inquiry questions, learning experiences/resources and assessment content carried from the scheme. Complete all required fields rather than assuming inherited text is a finished lesson plan.
5. Complete the template’s lesson details, links to other learning areas, relevant PCIs, learner support and other displayed preparation fields.
6. For each lesson phase, enter the duration, teacher activities and learner activities. Complete assessment criteria, learning points and stage resources as relevant.
7. Complete the assessment rubric shown in the form: at least three criteria with descriptions for all four performance levels.
8. Add an extended activity if appropriate and choose Save lesson plan.

- The plan is immediately usable.
- Do not wait for an admin approval button: lesson plans do not require approval.
- The “40-Min” label is not permission to enter a duration inconsistent with the actual scheduled session.

- Editing a lesson plan does not approve a new strand or expand approved scheme outcomes.
- Return to the curriculum and scheme review process if the approved content must change.

- After teaching, open the lesson to save its comment/reflection where available.
- This is useful lesson evidence, but it does not replace submitting a Record of Work for official coverage.

## 9. Document 4 — Record of Work

### Teacher: record what actually happened

1. Open Curriculum & Timetable → Records of Work and use the create/from-lesson action to open Record work from a lesson.
2. Check Class, Stream and Period. This creation form resolves the active operational year and term; it is not a way to submit future-period teaching. Ask leadership to resolve a wrong active period.
3. Select an Approved lesson. A detailed lesson plan is optional; an approved scheduled scheme session is sufficient.
4. Enter the Actual teaching date. The current form permits the scheduled date or a later date up to today. It does not permit future actual teaching dates or earlier-than-scheduled dates.
5. Choose Teaching status: COMPLETED, PARTIAL, POSTPONED or CANCELLED.
6. Check Outcomes actually covered. They start from the scheduled content: remove outcomes not actually achieved. Postponed or cancelled teaching should not claim covered outcomes.
7. Complete Remaining work / follow-up, Reflection and What did you actually teach? For a postponement/cancellation, explain what happened and the follow-up instead of claiming teaching took place.
8. Choose Save Record of Work draft.
9. Review the saved record and choose Submit for Review. A draft is not a submitted official record.

- You can record another eligible scheduled lesson while an earlier record awaits review.
- Do not create a duplicate active record for the same session.
- Use the existing record’s view/edit/resubmission controls instead.

- For PARTIAL teaching, describe exactly what remains and arrange follow-up through the scheme.
- The original record is retained; the whole scheduled lesson is not treated as completed simply because a partial record was approved.

### Leadership: review the teaching evidence

1. Open Records of Work, select the correct context and filter to Submitted records.
2. View the record and check teacher, scheduled source, actual date, work done, outcomes, teaching status, remaining work and reflection.
3. Choose Approve, review/add the approval remarks in the confirmation dialog and confirm, or choose Return and give a clear reason.
4. For returned work, the teacher reads Return Feedback, edits the record and chooses Resubmit.
5. Confirm approved records display Approved · Official and review coverage afterward.

- An authorised supervisor cannot approve a record identified as their own teaching record.
- Have another authorised leader review it.
- Do not use Delete to handle ordinary corrections: deletion removes teaching evidence and can affect progress.
- Use return/revision controls where available and preserve genuine history.

## 10. Read the progress correctly

- Open Syllabus Coverage Plan → Coverage progress.
- The progress view is read-only; change plans in the relevant document’s editing workflow.

- **Planned**
  - Meaning: A linked lesson plan has been prepared, according to the lesson’s planning status
- **Reported**
  - Meaning: Teaching has been submitted for review; it is not yet confirmed coverage
- **Confirmed / taught**
  - Meaning: Official approved teaching evidence confirms completion under the coverage rules
- **Reflected**
  - Meaning: The linked lesson’s reflection status has been recorded
- **Confirmed lesson coverage bar**
  - Meaning: Confirmed completed lessons divided by scheduled lessons in the selected view


- The bar uses maroon on a mild-grey track and displays the count and percentage.
- It is a lesson-completion percentage, not a percentage of all curriculum outcomes.
- Partial teaching must not be read as an entire completed lesson.
- Distinct confirmed outcomes are counted once within their relevant class/stream/learning-area/period scope even when taught repeatedly.
- Do not add reported and confirmed counts together as separate lessons; these indicators can describe different stages of the same session.

- If there are no scheduled lessons, the bar says No scheduled lessons.
- Check the matching timetable, calendar and generated scheme sessions before treating that as zero teacher effort.

## 11. View the three teaching documents

- Use the relevant View action in Schemes of Work, Lesson Plans and Records of Work.
- Check the school heading and the teacher/class/learning-area/year/term information before printing or sharing.
- Use the print/export option available in the document view; do not treat a screenshot of a dashboard as the official document.

- The scheme is a table of scheduled teaching content; the lesson plan contains preparation and lesson phases; the record of work shows actual work and reflection.
- A school heading identifies the school; it is not an approval signature.
- Check approval status separately.

## 12. Common problems and who should act

- **No assigned learning area**
  - Action: Leadership checks staff/login linkage, subject assignment, year, class, stream and effective dates; teacher refreshes assignments afterward
- **Assigned class but no planning subject**
  - Action: A class-teacher duty alone is insufficient; leadership checks subject-teacher duties
- **Missing curriculum**
  - Action: Check grade/learning-area filters; leadership publishes valid proposals; teacher chooses Refresh strands
- **Calendar not published / timetable missing**
  - Action: Administrator or Head Teacher completes the named setup for the matching period
- **Previous scheme missing**
  - Action: Leadership checks the named prior period; records genuine first use or resolves missing prior history truthfully
- **Approve button absent or disabled**
  - Action: Confirm reviewer role, selected context and SUBMITTED status; coverage approval must precede scheme approval; own-record approval is restricted
- **Scheme submit disabled**
  - Action: Save unsaved changes, complete required session fields, choose a valid submission scope and get source coverage approved
- **No lesson available for a lesson plan**
  - Action: Check approved scheme sessions for the selected assignment and period
- **No lesson available for a work record**
  - Action: Check active period, approval, scheduled date and whether that session already has an active record
- **Work saved but no confirmed progress**
  - Action: A draft must be submitted; submitted records must be approved; partial work does not confirm a whole lesson
- **A new holiday affects an approved lesson**
  - Action: Leadership publishes the exception; teacher and leadership review rescheduling without deleting approved history
- **“Record changed” or revision conflict**
  - Action: Reload the latest version, read changes and reapply only the needed correction; do not repeatedly submit an old screen


- Refresh the relevant list after another user approves something.
- Do not rely exclusively on notifications to discover a review decision.

## 13. Staff familiarisation exercise

- Use a local/disposable training school or clearly isolated training data.
- Do not create fictional production teaching records.

1. Leadership publishes one real curriculum strand/sub-strand with outcomes in the training context.
2. Configure the selected period, first-use/continuity decision, one subject assignment, a published calendar and its timetable.
3. Add a full-day holiday and a selected-period activity; confirm affected planning slots are excluded.
4. Teacher saves coverage for one sub-strand and submits it. Leadership returns it once with a specific comment; teacher corrects and resubmits; leadership approves.
5. Teacher completes one or more scheme sessions, saves and submits them; leadership approves the submitted sessions.
6. Teacher creates a lesson plan from an approved session and uses it immediately without approval.
7. Teacher saves and submits a completed Record of Work. Check reported progress before review; leadership approves; check confirmed progress afterward.
8. Repeat using another approved scheduled session without a detailed lesson plan. Submit it while an earlier record awaits review.
9. Record PARTIAL teaching and continue the sub-strand into another week. Confirm remaining work and the original record remain visible.
10. Test a later term with approved unfinished work and a separate later-term case with missing prior records. Confirm the correct carry-forward or administrator resolution is used.

- Before staff begin live work, leadership must confirm the operational year/term, actual assignments, published curriculum, first-use/continuity decision, teaching dates/exceptions and timetable.
- These are school decisions, not values teachers should guess.
